About

Work built around a simple question.

Adrian is a writer, founder, and public thinker whose work examines how people make decisions when information is abundant but context is missing.

He founded Lattice after observing that thoughtful people were often surrounded by reports, messages, and research, yet still struggled to turn information into direction. His work focuses on building systems that preserve context without replacing human judgment.

Adrian later established the Open Margin Fellowship, supporting emerging writers and creators during the uncertain early stages of a project. His essays and talks explore confidence, memory, authorship, and the relationship between people and the systems they create.

Outside the work

Adrian reads, walks, keeps extensive notes, and collects sentences heard in passing. He prefers ordinary cafés and restaurants to formal offices and has described a quiet booth near a window as his ideal place to think.

Work

Systems should reveal choices, not make them invisible.

01

LATTICE

One working picture for complex decisions.

Lattice is a knowledge and decision-support company built around a simple idea: most organizations do not lack information. They lack a way to see how their information connects.

Reports, conversations, research, and institutional memory often remain in separate places. Lattice brings them into one working picture so teams can understand the choices in front of them.

Lattice does not make decisions for people. It helps them see what a decision contains.

01

Gather context

Bring research, conversations, records, and previous decisions into one shared view.

02

Trace assumptions

Identify which conclusions are supported, inherited, missing, or simply repeated.

03

Preserve reasons

Record not only what was decided, but why that decision mattered at the time.

02

OPEN MARGIN

Room for work before it becomes certain.

Open Margin supports early-career writers, researchers, and independent creators who are developing work that is not yet ready to be defined.

Selected fellows receive time, editorial support, mentorship, and a small project grant. The program is designed for the stage when an idea is meaningful but its final form is still uncertain.

“We are not handing anyone a voice. We are making room for them to hear their own.”

EssayPrize winner · 2021

Borrowed Light

Confidence is often described as something we find inside ourselves. Adrian suggests that it may begin somewhere else: in another person’s willingness to believe in an idea before we can believe in it ourselves.

“Sometimes another person believes in an idea before you can believe in it yourself. You carry that confidence for a while, and eventually it becomes your own.”
